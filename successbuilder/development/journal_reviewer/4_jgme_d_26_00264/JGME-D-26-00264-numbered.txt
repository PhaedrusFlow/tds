P1:L19                                         historically been dominated by MD representation and spots for DO residents limited.
P1:L20                                         However, the ACGME merger that took place in 2020 eliminated this separation.
P1:L21                                         Objective: The aim of this study is to determine trends in DO representation in
P1:L22                                         competitive surgical specialties by evaluating changes in overall DO representation
P1:L23                                         and regional representation five years post-ACGME merger.
P1:L24                                         Methods: Utilizing publicly accessible data, DO representation was evaluated across
P1:L25                                         the following ACGME accredited competitive surgical specialties comparing PGY1 to
P1:L26                                         PGY5: ENT, general surgery, orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery, and urology. Data
P1:L27                                         was collected during the 2025 academic year. A total of 776 ACGME accredited
P1:L28                                         residency programs were evaluated.

P1:L32                                         found to have the most DO representation expansion with a 3% increase overall.

P1:L34                                         representation regionally in all specialties with the exception of orthopedic surgery and
P1:L35                                         urology. In conclusion, there was minimal overall DO representation advancement
P1:L36                                         across surgical specialties and regional distribution is varied.

P1:L221  8   Prior to the merger of the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) and the American
P1:L222  9
P1:L223 10   Osteopathic Association (AOA) accreditation systems that occurred in 2020, residency programs for allopathic
P1:L224 11
P1:L225 12   (MD) and osteopathic (DO) applicants were designated separately. During a five-year transition period from 2015-
P1:L226 13
P1:L227 14   2020, American Osteopathic Association (AOA) programs had to demonstrate compliance with ACGME
P1:L228 15
P1:L229 16   requirements. The resulting single accreditation system streamlines graduate medical education by eliminating
P1:L230 17
P1:L231 18   duplicative accreditation costs, ensuring uniform standards of evaluation and accountability, and expanding
P1:L232 19
P1:L233 20
P1:L234      opportunities for all medical graduates—including access to osteopathic training for both allopathic and osteopathic
P1:L235 21
P1:L236 22
P1:L237      students. It also fosters greater collaboration across the medical education community while providing transparency
P1:L238 23
P1:L239 24
P1:L240      for residents.¹

P1:L245 29   Competitive surgical specialties have historically been dominated by MD representation with spots for DO residents
P1:L246 30
P1:L247 31   limited. With the ACGME merge, this separation was eliminated, but the effects of this transition are not yet fully
P1:L248 32
P1:L249 33   understood. There are limited studies that detail a comprehensive analysis across multiple surgical disciplines and
P1:L250 34
P1:L251 35   geographic regions.
P1:L252 36
P1:L253 37
P1:L254 38
P1:L255 39   Prior studies reporting lower match rates for DO applicants compared to MD applicants in surgical fields. For
P1:L256 40
P1:L257 41   example, a 2022 study reported that nearly half of surveyed surgical programs did not train DO residents, 2 and an
P1:L258 42
P1:L259 43   additional study found lower DO match rates across multiple specialties.3 Similarly, a 2023 study demonstrated that
P1:L260 44
P1:L261 45   DO applicants experienced significant losses in ophthalmology following the merger. 4 Together, these findings
P1:L262 46
P1:L263 47   reinforce the persistent underrepresentation of DOs in highly competitive surgical fields. However, this study adds a
P1:L264 48
P1:L265 49   unique perspective to the current body of literature. Occurring five years after the ACGME merger this study is the
P1:L266 50
P1:L267 51   most recent assessment of its effects. These findings help draw attention to the current under representation of DO
P1:L268 52
P1:L269 53   students despite the ACGME merger. This highlights the need for further intervention to ensure equity between
P1:L270 54
P1:L271 55   allopathic and osteopathic students.
