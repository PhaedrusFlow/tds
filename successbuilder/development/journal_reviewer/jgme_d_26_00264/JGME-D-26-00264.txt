Keywords:                               Match Trends; Graduate medical education; Osteopathic; ACGME Merger;
                                        Representation; medical education; Geography; Regional Trends; Surgery;
                                        Competitive

Abstract:                               Background: Prior to the ACGME merger, residency programs were designated
                                        separately from MD and DO applicants. Competitive surgical specialties have
                                        historically been dominated by MD representation and spots for DO residents limited.
                                        However, the ACGME merger that took place in 2020 eliminated this separation.
                                        Objective: The aim of this study is to determine trends in DO representation in
                                        competitive surgical specialties by evaluating changes in overall DO representation
                                        and regional representation five years post-ACGME merger.
                                        Methods: Utilizing publicly accessible data, DO representation was evaluated across
                                        the following ACGME accredited competitive surgical specialties comparing PGY1 to
                                        PGY5: ENT, general surgery, orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery, and urology. Data
                                        was collected during the 2025 academic year. A total of 776 ACGME accredited
                                        residency programs were evaluated.
                                        Results: Based on the results, it was concluded that five years post ACGME merger,
                                        there was an increase in ENT and general surgery DO representation overall and a
                                        decrease in urology, orthopedic surgery, and plastic surgery. General surgery was
                                        found to have the most DO representation expansion with a 3% increase overall.
                                        Conclusion: In evaluating regional data, results indicate an increase in DO
                                        representation regionally in all specialties with the exception of orthopedic surgery and
                                        urology. In conclusion, there was minimal overall DO representation advancement
                                        across surgical specialties and regional distribution is varied.

 4                                          MAIN MANUSCRIPT: INTRODUCTION
 5
 6
 7
 8   Prior to the merger of the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) and the American
 9
10   Osteopathic Association (AOA) accreditation systems that occurred in 2020, residency programs for allopathic
11
12   (MD) and osteopathic (DO) applicants were designated separately. During a five-year transition period from 2015-
13
14   2020, American Osteopathic Association (AOA) programs had to demonstrate compliance with ACGME
15
16   requirements. The resulting single accreditation system streamlines graduate medical education by eliminating
17
18   duplicative accreditation costs, ensuring uniform standards of evaluation and accountability, and expanding
19
20
     opportunities for all medical graduates—including access to osteopathic training for both allopathic and osteopathic
21
22
     students. It also fosters greater collaboration across the medical education community while providing transparency
23
24
     for residents.¹
25
26
27
28
29   Competitive surgical specialties have historically been dominated by MD representation with spots for DO residents
30
31   limited. With the ACGME merge, this separation was eliminated, but the effects of this transition are not yet fully
32
33   understood. There are limited studies that detail a comprehensive analysis across multiple surgical disciplines and
34
35   geographic regions.
36
37
38
39   Prior studies reporting lower match rates for DO applicants compared to MD applicants in surgical fields. For
40
41   example, a 2022 study reported that nearly half of surveyed surgical programs did not train DO residents, 2 and an
42
43   additional study found lower DO match rates across multiple specialties.3 Similarly, a 2023 study demonstrated that
44
45   DO applicants experienced significant losses in ophthalmology following the merger. 4 Together, these findings
46
47   reinforce the persistent underrepresentation of DOs in highly competitive surgical fields. However, this study adds a
48
49   unique perspective to the current body of literature. Occurring five years after the ACGME merger this study is the
50
51   most recent assessment of its effects. These findings help draw attention to the current under representation of DO
52
53   students despite the ACGME merger. This highlights the need for further intervention to ensure equity between
54
55   allopathic and osteopathic students.

 4   In this study, we aim to further evaluate representation of DO graduates across five ACGME-accredited competitive
 5
 6   surgical specialties: ENT, general surgery, orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery, and urology. Understanding the
 7
 8   distribution of DO physicians in these fields can provide valuable insight into the opportunities for osteopathic
 9
10   graduates to match into competitive surgical specialties compared to their MD counterparts. This data can be used to
11
12   help future applicants, medical school advisors, and residency program directors better understand the trends that
13
14   exist for their specialty of interest.

29   Residency program data were collected from the American Association of Medical Colleges (AAMC) website for

37   From the listings of programs, MD and DO degree data was obtained in the following manner: Each individual

46

11                                              MAIN MANUSCRIPT: DISCUSSION
12
13
14
15   Overall, the findings suggest limited benefits of the ACGME merger for osteopathic applicants in competitive
16
17   surgical specialties. Although DO representation increased in general surgery and otolaryngology, decreases in
18
19   orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery, and urology underscore persistent differences. These results challenge the
20
21   ACGME’s assertion that the merger “enhanced opportunities” for DO applicants, indicating that systemic barriers
22
23   may remain.
24
25
26
27   The results of this study found that there was an increase in overall representation for DO applicants in ENT and
28
29   General Surgery. However, there was a decrease in overall representation in Orthopedic Surgery, Plastic Surgery,
30
31
     and Urology. These overall trends are evident in Figure 1. Regional changes were found to not be statistically
32
33
     significant in ENT, Orthopedic Surgery, Plastic Surgery, and Urology. However, General Surgery had an increase in
34
35
     DO representation in every region except the East North Central which showed a decrease. The largest
36
37
38   representation for General Surgery was in the East North Central region and the least in the New England region for
39
40   both PGY1 and PGY5 residents. Overall regional trends were also identified, with overall regional DO distribution
41
42   remaining highest in the East North Central region and lowest in the New England Region, no change was noted in
43
44   the Pacific and Mountain regions, however redistribution was noted in the West North Central, West South Central,
45
46   East South Central, and South Atlantic regions as appreciated in Figure 2.
47
48
49
50   Although there was the largest increase in representation in General Surgery overall and regionally, the specialty
51
52   itself is the least competitive of those analyzed. The increase in representation in the ENT group is far outweighed
53
54   by the decrease in representation in the Orthopedic Surgery, Plastic Surgery, and Urology groups.
55
56
57
58   Prior research has consistently shown lower match rates and underrepresentation of DO applicants in competitive
59
60   surgical specialties, with some studies noting significant post-merger declines. A study published in 2025 compared
61
62
63
64                                                                                                                          7
65
 1
 2
 3
 4   match rates of DO students before and after the ACGME merger. This study aligned with our results showing a
 5
 6   significant increase in DO match rates in general surgery, but no change in integrated residency positions like plastic
 7
 8   surgery and thoracic surgery. Our study, conducted five years after the ACGME merger, provides the most recent
 9
10   assessment and underscores the ongoing need for interventions to promote equity between MD and DO applicants.
11
12
13
14   While this study expands upon the current literature it is important to acknowledge certain limitations. One such
15
16   limitation is the collection of data from only five surgical specialties. There are other competitive surgical specialties
17
18   such as Neurosurgery, Vascular Surgery, Obstetrics and Gynecology, Cardiothoracic Surgery, and Ophthalmology
19
20
     which may have provided more insight to our study. Another constraint was the limited access to resident
21
22
     information for certain programs. These programs were excluded from the study but may have increased the
23
24
     statistical power. Finally, since there was an increase in representation among ENT and General Surgery the data
25
26
     may be interpreted differently and as increasing representation for DOs.

                                               MAIN MANUSCRIPT: CONCLUSIONS
32
33
34
35   Following the ACGME merger completed five years ago, osteopathic medical students have seen
36
37   minimal improvement in representation throughout the exploration of the five surgical specialties
38
39   detailed in our investigation with significant variation in regional distribution. Although increases
40
41   were observed in otolaryngology and general surgery, decreases in orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery,
42
43   and urology highlight ongoing differences. Regional analysis revealed heterogeneous trends, with
44
45   some areas showing modest growth while others demonstrated declines or stagnation. Further research
46
47   should evaluate additional competitive specialties, program-level factors such as leadership
48
49   composition, and applicant characteristics associated with successful DO matches. Our hope is that as
50
51   the number of DO applicants increase, we see a linear relationship in this representation in
52
53   competitive surgical specialties.
