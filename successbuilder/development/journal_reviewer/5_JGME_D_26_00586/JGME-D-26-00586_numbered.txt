P1:L17  Abstract:                             Background: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored in
P1:L18                                        academic medicine, but their reliability for substantive research evaluation remains
P1:L19                                        uncertain.
P1:L20                                        Objective: This study assessed the performance of three LLMs in evaluating pathology
P1:L21                                        research posters compared with a human faculty expert.
P1:L22                                        Methods: In this 2026 study, one pathology faculty member and three LLMs (ChatGPT-
P1:L23                                        5.2, Gemini-3, and Claude Sonnet-4.5) independently evaluated 90 de-identified
P1:L24                                        posters using a standardized rubric. The rubric included a modified poster type
P1:L25                                        classification and three ordinal domains: methods strength, conclusion validity, and
P1:L26                                        writing quality, each scored on a 1–5 scale. Inter-rater reliability was assessed using
P1:L27                                        Krippendorff’s α, Gwet’s AC2, and weighted kappa.
P1:L28                                        Results: Poster type classification showed perfect agreement across raters. In contrast,
P1:L29                                        agreement for ordinal domains was limited. Four-way exact agreement ranged from
P1:L30                                        0% for total score to 24.4% for conclusion validity, while mean pairwise agreement
P1:L31                                        ranged from 23.1% to 57.8%. Weighted kappa values were low across domains,
P1:L32                                        ranging from 0.058 to 0.226, with relatively higher agreement for writing quality.
P1:L33                                        Agreement among LLMs consistently exceeded agreement between LLMs and the
P1:L34                                        human evaluator. Scoring patterns also differed: ChatGPT assigned perfect scores
P1:L35                                        across all three ordinal domains to 12.22% of posters, compared with 5.56% for
P1:L36                                        Claude, 4.44% for Gemini, and none by the human evaluator.
P1:L37                                        Conclusion: LLMs showed strong reliability for categorical classification but limited
P1:L38                                        consistency in ordinal research evaluation. These findings support their use as
P1:L39                                        adjunctive tools rather than replacements for expert human reviewers.

P1:L67                Scholarly activity is a cornerstone of ACGME-accredited training programs, and program

P1:L69                research presentations (including poster projects) as part of milestone-based competency

P1:L73                study directly addresses this gap. We examined how three LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude
P1:L74                Sonnet) performed in evaluating ninety de-identified pathology student/resident research posters
P1:L75                against a standardized rubric, and we benchmarked their performance against an experienced
P1:L76                faculty rater using multiple inter-rater reliability indices (Krippendorff’s α, Gwet’s AC2, and
P1:L77                weighted κ).

P1:L79                Our findings carry important implications for GME programs considering AI-assisted evaluation
P1:L80                of trainee scholarly work: (1) All raters achieved perfect agreement on categorical poster-type
P1:L81                classification, suggesting LLMs can reliably handle discrete categorization tasks; (2) Ordinal
P1:L82                domain scoring agreement was uniformly low across all rater pairs, with pairwise weighted κ
P1:L83                values ranging from 0.058 to 0.226; (3) AI–AI pairs consistently outperformed human–AI pairs,

P1:L85                and (4) Scoring inflation varied meaningfully across models, with ChatGPT assigning perfect
P1:L86                scores to 12.22% of projects versus 0% for faculty. Together, these findings caution against
P1:L87                deploying LLMs as autonomous evaluators of trainee research and argue for their use as adjunct
P1:L88                tools that augment—rather than replace—expert faculty review.

P1:L90                We believe this manuscript makes a timely and substantive contribution to the literature on AI in
P1:L91                medical education. It provides actionable guidance for program directors, faculty evaluators, and
P1:L92                GME leaders considering LLM-assisted scholarly activity workflows, and it highlights the need
P1:L93                for improved rubric design and standardized prompting protocols before such tools can be
P1:L94                responsibly integrated into trainee assessment.

P1:L872  5
P1:L873  6
P1:L874  7
P1:L875  8   This study evaluated the reliability of three LLMs (ChatGPT-5.2, Gemini 3, and Claude
P1:L876  9
P1:L877 10   Sonnet 4.5) relative to a human faculty rater in scoring 90 pathology research posters
P1:L878 11
P1:L879 12
P1:L880 13   across four domains: Poster Type, Methods Strength, Conclusion Validity, and Quality of
P1:L881 14
P1:L882 15   Writing. A striking divergence emerged between categorical and ordinal performance. All
P1:L883 16
P1:L884 17
P1:L885 18   raters achieved perfect agreement on Poster Type classification, confirming that LLMs
P1:L886 19
P1:L887 20   can reliably replicate expert categorical judgment when criteria are well-defined and
P1:L888 21
P1:L889 22
P1:L890 23
P1:L891      mutually exclusive consistent with prior literature11,15–18 and suggesting a role for these
P1:L892 24
P1:L893 25   models in early-stage screening and administrative review7,19, 20. Ordinal scoring
P1:L894 26
P1:L895 27   presented a contrasting perspective. Four-way exact agreement ranged from only 13.3%
P1:L896 28
P1:L897 29
P1:L898 30   to 24.4% across the quality domains, with weighted kappa values falling in the slight-to-
P1:L899 31
P1:L900 32   fair range for all rater pairs. The composite total score showed the highest chance-
P1:L901 33
P1:L902 34
P1:L903 35   corrected agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.125; Gwet's AC2 = 0.702), a pattern partly
P1:L904 36

P1:L906 38
P1:L907 39
P1:L908 40   classification. A notable discrepancy between the two agreement indices was noted.
P1:L909 41
P1:L910 42   Krippendorff's α yielded very low values for ordinal domains (range: 0.013–0.062), while
P1:L911 43
P1:L912 44
