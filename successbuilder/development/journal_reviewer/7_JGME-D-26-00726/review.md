## Comment 3 — p. 5 | lines 4–24; p. 5 | lines 51–53

Please provide a fully reproducible protocol for publication and academic-appointment ascertainment, including search date(s), exact search strings, sources consulted, source hierarchy, procedures for reconciling discrepant records, and the number and independence of reviewers performing abstraction. Clarify whether the authors limited publications to PubMed-indexed records or also included non-indexed work and preprints. This is important because NLM’s NIH Preprint Pilot makes eligible NIH-supported preprints from medRxiv, bioRxiv, arXiv, and Research Square discoverable in PubMed, which provides the `preprint[pt]` publication-type filter to include or exclude them. This issue is relevant to the authors’ specialty and institution: PubMed indexes the medRxiv preprint, “The polygenic architecture of hidradenitis suppurativa reveals signaling mechanisms that implicate epithelial remodeling”, available at https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.25.25332168v1 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40766141/, with affiliations in NYU Langone Health’s Ronald O. Perelman Department of Dermatology and Department of Population Health. Although preprints should remain distinct from peer-reviewed publications, a PubMed-indexed preprint is a more stable and auditable scholarly record than LinkedIn or unarchived professional webpages because it includes persistent identifiers, dates, structured author information, affiliations, and a publication-type designation. Please state whether preprints were excluded, included, or reported separately, and, if included, how preprint–journal article pairs were identified and counted only once. Also describe procedures for author disambiguation, including use of full names, middle initials, training history, affiliations, coauthors, publication topics, and ORCID or other identifiers, with a prespecified rule for unresolved matches. Define “academic appointment,” including treatment of adjunct, volunteer, affiliate, courtesy, research-only, teaching-only, administrative, and nonclinical titles, and explain how historical 5- and 10-year status was verified; current webpages alone cannot establish past appointment status without archived or dated evidence. Finally, report whether abstraction was duplicated, how uncertain records and disagreements were adjudicated, and any agreement statistic calculated; if one reviewer abstracted data, report this as a limitation.

## Comment 4 — p. 5 | lines 12–34; Table 1

The authors define follow-up as beginning after residency or, for participants who pursued fellowship, after fellowship. This creates different index dates and may classify work produced during fellowship differently across participants. Please report fellowship frequency, type, and duration by exposure group; justify this definition; and consider a sensitivity analysis using a common post-residency index date or accounting for fellowship participation and duration. The 10-year academic-appointment endpoint also requires clarification. The authors state that participants who had not reached 10 years after training were “not applicable,” yet Table 1 labels 19 of 45 participants in the 4-year group and 15 of 31 in the 5–6-year group as “Missing.” Administrative ineligibility or right-censoring should be distinguished from missing data. Among apparently eligible participants, the table shows 10 appointments among 26 participants in the 4-year group (38.5%) and 7 among 16 in the 5–6-year group (43.8%), rather than the displayed full-group percentages of 22.2% and 22.6%. Please report eligible denominators, explain the Fisher exact P value using eligible counts, and clarify how participants with incomplete 5–10-year follow-up contributed to papers-per-year estimates.

## Comment 5 — p. 5 | lines 39–53; Table 1; Table 2

The medical-school-duration groups are independent, but the authors use “paired analysis,” “paired year analyses,” and “paired years” in the Methods, Results, and Table 2. If the intended meaning is pairwise comparisons between independent duration groups, please use that terminology instead. The authors identify Kruskal-Wallis, Wilcoxon rank-sum, and Fisher exact tests in the Methods and Table 1 footnote, but Table 2 reports mean differences, confidence intervals, and Scheffé-adjusted P values. Please specify the model used to estimate Table 2 differences and intervals; the omnibus test preceding post hoc comparisons; the family of comparisons receiving the Scheffé adjustment; whether the adjustment was prespecified; how that analysis relates to the nonparametric tests; and why the chosen model is appropriate given the skewed distributions shown in Table 1. Table 2 also appears internally inconsistent: for 5–6 years minus 4 years in papers per year during years 0–10, the estimate is 0.062, the lower confidence limit is also 0.062, the upper limit is 1.026, and the adjusted P value is 0.988. An interval lying wholly above zero is not compatible with a two-sided P value near 1 if both arise from the same contrast. The authors should verify the estimate, confidence interval, and P value directly from the statistical output.

## Comment 6 — p. 6 | lines 4–26; p. 8 | lines 12–19

The authors report no statistically significant differences between the 4-year and 5–6-year groups and then state that detecting a difference of 1 paper per year with 80% power would require 64 participants per group, whereas their groups include 45 and 31 participants. By the authors’ own calculation, the study lacks the stated power to detect that effect. The authors should therefore distinguish “no statistically significant difference was detected” from “there is no difference,” emphasize effect estimates and corrected confidence intervals, and acknowledge that meaningful effects remain compatible with these data. The conclusion that completing a research year “does not portend increased academic productivity” is too definitive given the selected cohort, limited sample size, and unverified exposure definition.

## Comment 7 — p. 6 | lines 4–48; Table 2

The authors include the 7-year group in Table 2 and report the only statistically significant comparison for this group, but do not characterize it in the Results. Its size is inferable as 20 participants from the total sample of 96 minus the 45- and 31-participant groups, but should be reported directly. Please describe the verified reasons for additional years and the distribution of research years, MD/PhD or other degrees, leave, and other pathways in this group. The authors also evaluate multiple outcomes, time windows, authorship categories, and pairwise duration-group comparisons. Please define the multiplicity family receiving the Scheffé adjustment and clarify whether the adjustment was prespecified and applied across outcomes, within each outcome, or only across the three duration-group contrasts. Until the analytic model, adjustment family, and 7-year-group composition are clear, the isolated adjusted P value of 0.019 should be interpreted cautiously.
